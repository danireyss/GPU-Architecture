The big idea: each byte has to carry enough math, or memory sets the speed.

  • 500 GB/s and 20 trillion operations a second are the course's example chip, not one real product. So are the bigger card's 1,000 GB/s and 30 trillion.
  • The board rounds 41.7 billion to 42. This page shows 41.7, because the model gives 500 ÷ 12 = 41.67.
Where the numbers come from
  • The model: one pure function, written and tested before drawing: elements a second = the smaller of bandwidth ÷ bytes per element and operations a second ÷ operations per element. Every number, bar, dot, marker and label on the board comes from it.
  • Real: 384 wires × 21 gigabits. That is the published memory of the GeForce RTX 4090, a 384-bit bus of GDDR6X at 21 Gbps. 384 × 21 ÷ 8 = 1,008 GB/s, which rounds to about 1,000. Spec sheets also list it as 1,008 or about 1,010.
  • Not checked: 30 trillion operations a second. It comes from your brief. A real card's figure depends on what counts as an operation, and this page does not claim it matches the RTX 4090.
  • Busy means the share of the lanes' capacity in use, not power or a utilisation meter. A tool such as nvidia-smi reports something different: a GPU can show 100% while its lanes wait on memory like this.
  • What the model leaves out: every byte here crosses the pipe, once. Real caches let a kernel that reuses data do much more math per byte than this suggests, and a bus loses time to refresh and to switching between reads and writes, so 500 GB/s is a peak rather than a sustained rate.
  • One operation is one lane doing one thing for one clock. Real kernels mix cheap and expensive operations.
  • New kernel operation counts are round figures chosen for the figure, not measurements. The Mandelbrot kernel reads nothing and takes 8 operations for each of about 100 steps. The n-body kernel takes 20 for each of 1,000 neighbours and assumes no caching.
  • The pipe's two directions are drawn as two lanes for clarity. Real memory shares its wires between reads and writes, which is why the model adds them together. The dots run at 1 for each 10 GB a second: their spacing is true, and the speed along the pipe means nothing.

Sources for the 384 × 21 figure: TechPowerUp RTX 4090 review, Notebookcheck RTX 4090 specs.
