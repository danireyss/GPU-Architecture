The big idea: threads on different banks are served together; threads on one bank wait in line.

  • The model assumes 32 banks of 4-byte words, as on recent NVIDIA GPUs. 8-byte accesses and other vendors work differently.
  • The rule has one exception. cycles = gcd(stride, 32) holds for strides from 1 up, and was checked against the model for 1 to 200. All threads reading one word is stride 0, where gcd(0, 32) is 32 but the read takes 1 cycle, because identical words count once.
  • The extra grey column is on the tile. In the bank grid those same 32 padding words land on a diagonal, because each one's bank is still its index mod 32.

Where the numbers come from
  • The model: one pure function, written and tested before anything was drawn. A word's bank is its index mod 32, and a warp read takes as many cycles as the largest number of different words requested from any one bank. Identical words count once. The counters, queues, lines and table are all driven by its output.
  • Why gcd: the words st for t = 0 to 31 fall in 32 ÷ gcd(s, 32) banks, with gcd(s, 32) threads in each. So 2 gives 16 banks of 2, 3 gives every bank once, 32 gives one bank of 32, and 33 behaves like 1.
  • Real, from secondary sources: 32 banks, successive 32-bit words in successive banks, different addresses in one bank serialised, identical addresses broadcast with no conflict, and the a[32][33] padding fix are all described in NVIDIA conference slides, course notes and blog posts. I could not reach the current CUDA guides, and much of this material dates from Fermi to Kepler. Compute capability 1.x GPUs had 16 banks. Check the current guide before relying on it for a specific GPU.
  • A cycle here is one pass of the banks, not total latency. A real load also has a fixed pipeline latency that this model leaves out, so "1 cycle" means no extra waiting from bank conflicts, not that the value arrives one clock later.
  • Padding: 33 wide stores 33 × 32 = 1,056 words: 32 more words, 128 bytes, 3.1% of the 4 KB tile. Each row starts one bank later than the row above, so a column runs diagonally across all 32 banks.
  • Bank conflicts only happen within one warp's instruction. The model has one warp and one load. The order threads pass within a bank is a choice made for the drawing.
  • Made up for the figure: the tile's picture is a placeholder, and the broadcast word is word 7. Any single word gives the same answer. Loading the tile is not timed.
  • Grid depth: the board shows only the 33 rows this tile needs. Real shared memory is much deeper, with tens of kilobytes or more per SM.

Sources: NVIDIA GTC talk, Lei Mao on shared memory banks, Cornell Virtual Workshop, Virginia Tech lecture slides.
