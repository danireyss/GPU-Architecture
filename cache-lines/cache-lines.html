The big idea: a cache moves whole lines, so neighbours come along free.

  • 16 lines is a toy size, chosen so eviction is visible. A real GPU L1 holds on the order of a hundred kilobytes, so about a thousand lines.
  • Hit and miss latencies are left out on purpose. This model counts lines and bytes, not time, and the speed of the animation means nothing.

Where the numbers come from
  • The model: one pure function, written and tested before anything was drawn. It reads a list of addresses into a 16-line cache with 128-byte lines and least-recently-used eviction. Every counter, display, chip and table row comes from its output, and the trays land where it says.
  • Fetched and used: fetched is misses × 128 bytes. Used counts each 4-byte cell the pass asked for once. Useful is used ÷ fetched.
  • Why re-reading every 32nd misses: the pass touches 320 lines in a loop, the rack holds 16, and LRU evicts the line that will come back soonest in this pattern. No line survives until its turn comes round.
  • Real, and not the whole story: 128-byte lines. NVIDIA forum answers and an H100 course slide describe L1 lines of 128 bytes, each split into four 32-byte sectors with its own status bits, and a miss fetches only the sectors it needs. So on real hardware a miss can bring 32 bytes rather than the whole 128, and this page's every-32nd case would be about 12.5% useful rather than 3.1%. The idea survives at a smaller grain: neighbours within a sector come along free. These are secondary sources and the details vary by GPU.
  • Fully associative: any line can sit in any slot, which is what lets one LRU order cover all 16. So no address bits pick the rack slot: the tag is compared against all 16 at once, and the other field of the address picks the slot in the tray. Real L1s are set-associative, and the associativity and replacement policy vary and are mostly not published.
  • 32 threads to a warp is standard for NVIDIA GPUs. "Scattered" here means 32 different lines of a separate array, in a fixed shuffled order.
  • Made up for the figure: the two-digit values in the cells are placeholders, and the addresses of the arrays are chosen so that they share no lines. Reads run one at a time.
  • Left out: L2 and the memory controller, which a real miss goes through; prefetching; writes (a dropped tray here is simply gone, because VRAM still has the copy); several misses in flight on the same line.
  • The strip slides only so the figure can show the line in reach. VRAM is not a tape, and any line can be reached directly. The address map on the bed behind the strip is to scale: 512 ticks, one per line of the 16-bit space.

Sources for the sector detail: NVIDIA forum, cacheline and sector, NVIDIA forum, L1 and L2 granularity, H100 course slides.
